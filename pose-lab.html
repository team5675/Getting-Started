Pose lab

Move sliders to see what a camera mistake, a trust setting or a late picture does to the robot’s idea of where it is.

What this page is for

Try the ideas from Advanced 4–6 with sliders, and see the result immediately: a camera mount mistake, how much to trust the camera, and what a late picture does. Nothing to install; it runs in this page.

Before you start: Read Advanced 4, 5 and 6, or keep them open. Each panel says which page it goes with.

Allow 30–45 minutes. Try a prediction before each slider move.

A simplified, made-up world

This lab uses 2D math and invented numbers. It follows the same rules as the lessons (WPILib’s pose math and blending rule), but it is not the Limelight’s solver or the robot’s code. Use it to build intuition, then check real behavior on the robot and in its logs.

1. A camera mount mistake Advanced 4

A camera 0.30 m in front of the robot’s center looks at a tag. The code believes the camera points straight ahead. Turn it a little and see what each way of using the camera does.

Try this:

  1. With MegaTag1 and the camera turned 2°: predict the heading error, then check it. Move the robot closer and farther. What changes, and what doesn’t?
  2. Switch to MegaTag2. Now move the robot from x = 7 to x = 1. How does the position error grow?
  3. Choose “MegaTag2 after a MegaTag1 seed”. Why does the position error almost vanish, and what is still wrong?
  4. Set the turn to 0° and move the camera 5 cm forward. Does the error depend on distance now?

2. How much to trust the camera Advanced 5

A robot drives a figure-eight for 20 seconds. Its wheels slip a little, and at 8 seconds another robot shoves it sideways. The wheels don’t measure a shove, so odometry never finds out. Every 0.1 s the camera gives a noisy fix, and a few fixes are wildly wrong, like a single tag’s mirror-image answer.

Gray = real path · amber = odometry only · blue = odometry + camera · red dots = camera fixes · red × = mirror-image fixes

Share per fix–
Odometry-only error at the end–
Blended error, average (last 10 s)–
Blended error, worst (last 10 s)–

Try this:

  1. Set the shove to 0, then 60 cm. Watch the amber path jump away from the gray one and never come back. What does the blue path do?
  2. Untick “throw away ambiguous fixes”. What do the mirror-image fixes do to the blue path?
  3. Set the camera std dev very small (trust it a lot), then very large. Which gives the smallest average error? Which is jumpiest? There is a sweet spot, and it depends on the noise.

3. A late picture Advanced 6

A robot drives straight at a steady speed. Its odometry started 30 cm behind the truth. Camera fixes arrive every 0.04 s, each showing where the robot was when its picture was taken. One estimate uses the picture’s time; the other pretends each picture was taken “now”.

Error after 3 s, using picture time–
Error after 3 s, pretending “now”–

Try this:

  1. Predict the red line’s final error for 3 m/s and 50 ms. Hint: speed × latency.
  2. Set the speed to 0. Why do the two lines agree?
  3. Tick “wrong units”. The robot rejects every fix, and on the practice bot the vision.timestamp alert would fire. Which is better: rejecting them, or using them?
