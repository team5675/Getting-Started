Motor lab 3 · the team’s motor library

Let the TalonFX do the math

Run the same roller on a simulated TalonFX with Phoenix 6: your gains in Slot0, a velocity request and a current limit.

What you already did

You need your KS, KV and KP from Motor lab 1 and Motor lab 2, in the same sim-starter project. Allow 45–60 minutes.

You will prove: the same roller holds 20 rps on a simulated TalonFX using your three numbers, and its start-up current stays under a limit you set.

Mentor check before you start

This step adds a library to your project: CTRE’s Phoenix 6, version 26.3.0, the library the team’s robots use for TalonFX motor controllers. Your next build downloads it once, so be online for that build.

Phoenix 6 includes simulation files for every CTRE device, even ones this lab never uses. One of them is named CTRE_SimPigeonIMU.dll. If your antivirus reports or removes a CTRE file, stop and tell a mentor; do not change antivirus settings yourself.

Until you type .\robot talonfx, your project does not use Phoenix 6, so these files cannot affect your earlier labs. After it, every build of this project uses Phoenix 6, including the checks for earlier labs, until you undo this lab as step 1 describes. If a mentor decides this computer should not get CTRE’s files, skip this lab: do not type the command, and keep your MotorRoller from Motor lab 2.

1. Add the TalonFX files

Make a backup or Git checkpoint. Stop the simulation, then type:

.\robot talonfx

It copies two files under src and one library file, and never replaces a file you already have:

  • main/java/frc/robot/subsystems/TalonRoller.java: the roller on a TalonFX. Its TODOs are yours.
  • test/java/frc/robot/TalonLabTest.java: the checks for this lab.
  • vendordeps/Phoenix6-frc2026-latest.json: tells the build to use Phoenix 6, version 26.3.0.

If Gradle answers Task 'talonfx' not found, add the motor lab folders.

To undo this whole lab later, go back to the backup or Git checkpoint you just made. Without one, do all three of these:

  1. Delete those three files.
  2. In StudentProgram.java, delete the three lines step 6 adds.
  3. Remove the // that step 6 puts in front of your Motor lab 2 B line.

Deleting only the files is not enough: StudentProgram would still use TalonRoller, and the build would stop with cannot find symbol.

2. See who does the math now

In Motor lab 2, your robot code worked out the volts every 20 ms. A TalonFX can do that same math itself. Your code sends one request, “hold 20 rps”, and the TalonFX uses the gains stored in it to work out the volts, about 1000 times a second, right beside the motor.

Read the top of TalonRoller.java. Three supplied lines matter:

  • VelocityVoltage is a control request: “hold this speed, using volts”. Its .withVelocity(...) sets the target.
  • NeutralOut is a request too: “stop driving the motor”.
  • config.Feedback.SensorToMechanismRatio = RollerPhysics.GEARING; tells the TalonFX the motor turns 3 times per roller turn. So the TalonFX measures, and you give targets, in roller rotations per second, the same units as your constants.

3. TODO 1 · send the request

Replace the placeholder return in runAtSpeed:

return runEnd(() -> {
    this.targetRps = targetRps;
    motor.setControl(speedRequest.withVelocity(targetRps));
}, () -> {
    this.targetRps = 0.0;
    motor.setControl(stopRequest);
}).withName("TalonFX hold " + targetRps + " rps");

Compare it with your Motor lab 2 command: the shape is the same. Only the line inside changed, from “set these volts” to “hold this speed”.

4. TODO 2 · give Slot0 your three numbers

In the constructor, under the TODO 2 comment, add:

config.Slot0.kS = MotorRoller.KS_VOLTS;
config.Slot0.kV = MotorRoller.KV_VOLTS_PER_RPS;
config.Slot0.kP = MotorRoller.KP_VOLTS_PER_RPS;

Slot0 is the first of the TalonFX’s slots for gains. kS, kV and kP mean exactly what they meant in Motors 2, in the same units, so your own numbers go straight in. The supplied loop then sends the whole configuration to the TalonFX, trying a few times in case a message is lost.

5. TODO 3 · limit the current

Under the TODO 3 comment, add:

config.CurrentLimits.StatorCurrentLimit = CURRENT_LIMIT_AMPS;
config.CurrentLimits.StatorCurrentLimitEnable = true;

Motors 1 showed the start-up surge: a stopped motor can draw hundreds of amps. A stator current limit caps the current in the motor itself, at 60 A here. That protects the motor and wiring, and helps keep the battery from browning out the robot. Because current makes twist, the limit also caps how hard the roller can speed up. Every team motor configuration has current limits.

6. Connect it and compare

In StudentProgram.configure, add the TalonFX roller and move the B button to it:

frc.robot.subsystems.TalonRoller talon = new frc.robot.subsystems.TalonRoller(
        () -> robot.input().objectPresent());
robot.driver.b().whileTrue(talon.runAtSpeed(20.0));

Put // in front of the Motor lab 2 line that binds B. Keep your MotorRoller: TalonRoller uses its three constants.

  1. Start the simulation with .\robot. The first build downloads Phoenix 6, so it takes longer.
  2. Repeat the Motor lab 2 run: hold B for three seconds, press O while you keep holding B, hold two more seconds, then release B.
  3. Graph SpeedRps, StatorCurrentAmps and MotorVolts under Training/Talon.

Compare with your Motor lab 2 log. What does the current do at the start now? Does the roller take longer or shorter to reach 20 rps, and why? How big is the dip when the load arrives?

7. Check your implementation

Disable and stop the simulation, then run:

.\robot test --tests "frc.robot.TalonLabTest" --rerun-tasks

CTRE’s simulated TalonFX runs on the real clock, not the stepped clock of Motor labs 1 and 2, so this check takes a few seconds and allows a little more tolerance: within 1 rps of 20 after two seconds, current no more than 10 A over the limit, and within 1.5 rps one second after the load. Compare with talonfx-lab/answer only after trying.

Your turn · a gentler limit

Change CURRENT_LIMIT_AMPS to 30. Predict first: how much longer will the roller take to reach 20 rps, and will it still recover from the load? Run it, read the log, and explain the difference with the words current, twist and speeding up.

The supplied NeutralMode line chooses Coast or Brake for when the motor is stopped. This simulation does not model the difference. On real hardware, Brake stops a roller quickly and Coast lets it spin down; a mentor can show both.

Before you move on

Show the passing check and your comparison. Explain what Slot0 holds, what SensorToMechanismRatio changes, and why the current limit made the start slower.

Record one prediction, the actual result and your explanation in learning-notes.txt.

Connection to team code

The team’s drivetrain and mechanism code is built this way: CTRE’s swerve generator writes Slot0 gains and current limits for every drive and steering motor, and commands send control requests such as VelocityVoltage. You can now read those configurations and say what each number does.
